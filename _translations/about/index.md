# About Qifa Talk

## Who We Are

Qifa Talk is a nonprofit conversation group founded by members of Seattle's Chinese community. We are registered in Washington State and have received federal tax-exempt status from the IRS as a 501(c)(3) charitable organization. We aim to create an open, thoughtful, and equal space for Chinese-language conversation, where people with different backgrounds and experiences can discuss the questions they care about in depth.

Our online and in-person gatherings cover philosophy, society, science, culture, history, technology, and more. We encourage everyone to express their views freely, listen carefully, and discuss ideas with attention to facts, logic, and mutual respect. We value asking questions, reflecting, and understanding one another more than finding a single correct answer. We welcome both common ground and constructive disagreement: encounters between different perspectives can lead to new understanding.

One of our inspirations is the iQIYI debate show *Qi Pa Shuo*, which approaches contemporary questions with humor and makes critical thinking engaging. We hope to bring that spirit to Seattle's Chinese community, connecting curious people who think independently and care about public life. Together, we are building a community that keeps learning, discussing freely, and inspiring one another.

We believe meaningful conversations help us see a wider world.

## Our Values

* **Openness**: Welcome people from different backgrounds and with different views.
* **Respect**: Discuss ideas rather than attack people; speak thoughtfully and listen carefully.
* **Curiosity**: Approach unfamiliar subjects with a desire to learn.
* **Connection**: Build real relationships through the exchange of ideas.

## What We Do

Since our founding, we have held more than **20 events**, including:

* **Debates**: Structured discussions with opposing sides.
* **Talks and Sharing**: Members present topics that interest them.
* **Movie Nights**: Film screenings followed by discussion of deeper themes.
* **Outdoor Gatherings**: Walk through parks and talk along the way.

Past topics include responsibility for previous generations' historical actions, depression and public understanding, World War II, and a Valentine's Day special.

## Our Standard Debate Format

* **Discuss ideas with your team**: 15 minutes.
* **Brief introductions**: 10 minutes.
* **Opening arguments**: Up to 3 minutes per speaker, alternating sides; 30 minutes overall.
* **Free debate**: Up to 2 minutes per turn, with 30 minutes per side; 60 minutes overall.
* **One-on-one debate**: Either side may initiate; each person has 3 minutes in total.
* **Commentators** share their observations and perspectives.
* **Informal debrief**: Which moments stood out? What else would you like to add?
